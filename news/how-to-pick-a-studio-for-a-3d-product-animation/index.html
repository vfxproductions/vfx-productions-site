PRODUCT ANIMATION

How to pick a studio for a 3D product animation

Current rates span from 5k to 25k per finished minute. We mapped who does what work and at what price point.

11 September 2026 / Vision For Xperiences Studio Desk / 3 min read
3d animationproduct visualizationstudio selectionvideo productionpricing

A minute of 3D product animation costs anywhere from 5,000 to 25,000 USD in 2026, depending on who you hire and what photoreal means to you. The gap is real: a freelancer on Upwork charges differently than The Mill. A small senior team works differently than a 200-person agency. Before you call anyone, know what you're actually shopping for.

the pricing tiers

Freelancers and small remote studios land in the 3,000 to 7,000 per minute band. That's 30-second product rotations, simple explainers, basic motion graphics. Work gets done, but you're managing the relationship and often waiting. Mid-range studios (The Mill, Framestore, DNEG) charge 12,000 to 20,000 per minute and include broadcast-grade rendering, look development, and a project manager. Top-tier cinematic work exceeds 25,000 per minute.

But per-minute math can mislead. A 30-second hero shot might cost 5,000 to 15,000 flat. A 60-second explainer with multiple camera passes costs 8,000 to 25,000. Most studios now quote by project scope, not runtime, because a five-second product reveal with perfect materials and lighting can take as long as 30 seconds of walking animation.

who does what

The Mill, Framestore, and DNEG are full-service broadcast producers. They handle modeling, materials, lighting, animation, and compositing under one roof. They work on film campaigns and automotive launches. Call them when budget is secure and you need uncompromising photoreal.

Ufo3D, 3D-ACE, and Juego Studios specialise in product work specifically. They know the constraints of e-commerce visualization, AR product tours, and animated teardowns. They move faster than broadcast houses on product-specific briefs. Vision For Xperiences sits here too: direct contact with the animators, working across budget bands from 1,000 to 15,000+ EUR, and no middleman layer between you and the people making the work.

Buck leans design-first, if your product needs brand storytelling, not just rotation. Prolific Studio is US-focused and carries a mid-market brand.

how studios differ beyond price

Tools matter less than process. Maya, Cinema 4D, and Blender all produce photorealistic work now. What actually separates studios is turnaround (some quote four weeks, some quote two months), revision policy (are unlimited revisions included or charged per round), and whether look development is in scope or billed separately.

When you approach a studio, describe your product (complexity, materials, surface finish), your shot list (rotating hero? exploded view? in-context use?), and your timeline. Good studios ask more questions than you do. They know that a 14-day turnaround means different scoping than 8 weeks, and that automotive paint is not the same engineering problem as jewelry or electronics.

the one question that matters

Do you need photorealism, or do you need visual clarity? A luxury watch needs perfect materials and lighting to sell. A technical device or software interface might work better in a clean, stylised render where the product reads clearly at any size. That choice changes which studio makes sense and what you pay.

Quick answers

Can I get 3D product animation under 5,000?

Yes, from freelancers and small studios for shorter clips (15–30 seconds) or simpler subjects. Scope shrinks with budget. Vision For Xperiences takes projects from 1,000 EUR, but expect constraints on rounds of revision and render complexity below 5,000. Be specific about shot count and product complexity when you brief.

How long does a 3D product animation actually take?

Modeling and setup take 2–3 weeks. Animation and iteration take 1–2 weeks. Rendering and final passes take 1 week. Total: 4–6 weeks for a finished minute, longer if you request multiple hero shots or environment work. Turnaround shrinks if scope shrinks or budget allows parallel teams.

Is Blender cheaper than Maya or Cinema 4D?

Blender is free, but the cost difference between tools barely matters. You pay for artists, not software licenses. A studio using Blender charges what the market bears for the quality. The real cost difference is between hiring one senior animator (expensive, fast) versus a team of juniors (cheaper, slower).

What's the difference between product animation and product rendering?

Rendering is static: a single photorealistic image of your product from one angle. Animation adds motion: rotating the product, showing how it works, or placing it in context. Animation costs more but sells harder because buyers see the product in 3D, not flat.
