MOTION CAPTURE

Markerless mocap just became free, and the stage disappeared

Epic and Move AI shipped better video-based motion capture this summer. The tools are fast enough that a phone camera in a room becomes the whole rig.

01 October 2026 / Vision For Xperiences Studio Desk / 3 min read
animationmocapmarkerlessvideo capturereal-time

Epic Games shipped a free markerless motion capture plugin for Unreal Engine 5.8 in June, and Move AI followed with the M3 model in July. Both pull clean body animation directly from video. No markers. No suit. No dedicated space.

The math changed because the solvers got better. Move AI's M3 handles multiple performers at once, runs faster on standard hardware, and the video just needs to be clear enough to see a body moving. That threshold is not "professional shoot with three Alexa cameras". It is "a phone in a room" or "a webcam propped on a shelf". Autodesk Flow Studio added a static-camera mode in May that stabilises ground-plane footage, making single-angle captures actually usable for full-body work.

The catch is obvious: hand and finger detail still suffers when there is not enough resolution or angle coverage. Markerless capture works brilliantly for a walk cycle or a gesture. For a hand closing around an object, you still want better data. The free Epic plugin is a blocker-and-polish tool, not a delivery system. If the motion matters, you will spend time cleaning passes in a graph.

But that shift moves the cost calculus. We built a live visuals system for a festival where timing and sync required tight control over what played back when. Blocking and previs for that kind of work used to mean booking time on someone else's mocap floor or booking an inertial suit rental. Now the question is different: can we shoot it in the space we are already in, get data into Unreal Engine fast enough to iterate, and hand off something good? If the answer is yes, the budget and timeline open up.

The industry shifted this year. Move 4D published an analysis in September saying markerless is no longer the alternative for real-world work, it became the standard. The market for the whole space is projected to grow 17.3% annually through 2035, and the growth is all on the software side. The mocap suit is not dead, but it is no longer the default.

For character animation where you need broadcast-grade data, the old path still exists. XSens inertial systems still ship clean motion without line-of-sight problems. If you are shooting a commercial where the talent is rotating or partially occluded, a suit with gyros and accelerometers still wins. The hybrid studios that do this work know the tradeoffs and own the gear.

Vision For Xperiences works in the space where blocking and real-time animation intersect, and this changes the approach. The workflow would be: shoot in the location, capture in the afternoon, iterate on the motion in engine the same evening, and hand over polished data by next morning. That timeline is not how mocap works today for most shops. It is fast enough now that it is worth testing. The question for anyone making character work or interactive content is whether the first cut from video is clean enough to ship or clean enough to refine. For a lot of jobs, it is.

Quick answers

Can I use my phone camera for professional motion capture?

Yes, if the footage is clear and framed well. Epic's free plugin works on video from any source, and Move AI's M3 processes phone footage. Quality depends on resolution and lighting, but a modern phone is good enough for body capture. Hands and fingers need more angles to look great.

Do I need a mocap suit anymore?

No for many jobs, but yes for premium work. Inertial suits like XSens still deliver cleaner motion when talent is rotating, partially hidden, or needs broadcast-grade detail. Markerless video capture is cheaper and faster to set up, but trades detail and precision in complex situations.

How long does it take to process video into usable motion data?

The new solvers are fast enough that a capture shot in the morning can export clean FBX for use in-engine by afternoon. Move AI's M3 processes multiple performers at once on standard GPU hardware. Cleanup in a graph editor may still be needed for final polish.
