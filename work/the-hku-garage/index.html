Work / Music & Visuals

The HKU Garage

A 3D animation for the I Light U light festival in Utrecht, shown on the NEO screen above the entrance of Hoog Catharijne: a roller door in a painted wall opens onto a 3D-scanned garage, lit by the festival logo in neon.

Category
Music & Visuals
Year
2023
Client
I Light U

I Light U is the light festival of Utrecht, and one of its canvases is NEO, the LED screen across the front of Hoog Catharijne. The festival asked for an animation about light. We made “The HKU Garage”: ten seconds in which a familiar wall turns out to have something behind it. The roller door with the HKU lettering rolls up, and inside is a cluttered garage with a fridge, a bike up on the wall, and the I Light U logo glowing as a neon sign, which is the message the piece ends on.

The challenge

The idea started with a door. Kees Moolenaar, our CEO, studied at the HKU, and one of its locations at the Pastoe Fabriek has a roller door in a painted wall that stayed with him long after: the kind of door you walk past every day and wonder about. The brief was light, and the screen is not a normal format. NEO is very wide and low, 2674 by 964 pixels, and it is part of the glass front above a busy square, so the picture has to read in a second from far away. That asks for a simple idea: one wall, one door, one surprise.

The approach

Almost everything in the shot is real, just not from the same place. The wall and the door were photographed and scanned in 3D, so the mural has real depth when the light changes. What is behind the door comes from somewhere else entirely: a 3D scan of his grandfather’s garage, clutter and all, made a few weeks earlier. Putting the two together just made sense: a door everyone knows, and a room nobody expects, placed behind the wall as if it had always been there. A fridge and an old bike were added as props. The I Light U logo was redrawn as curves and turned into animated neon tubes that light the whole room, and the door is one surface that rolls up along a curve. Rendered in Blender Cycles at 60 frames per second. A viewport preview and two reference stills went to the festival before the final render.
